Prostate cancer: common drugs, usual doses and side effects

Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT): the backbone of almost every regimen, usually lifelong in metastatic disease

Drug (brand)Usual dose and scheduleCommon side effectsHospitalist pearls
Leuprolide (Lupron Depot, Eligard)7.5 mg q1 month, 22.5 mg q3 months, 30 mg q4 months, or 45 mg q6 months (IM or SC by product)Hot flashes, fatigue, low libido and erectile dysfunction, weight gain and loss of muscle, bone loss and fractures, higher glucose and lipids, anemia, mood change, breast tenderness, QTc prolongationDo not stop in hospital (note when the next injection is due). Starting a GnRH agonist causes a testosterone flare for 1–3 weeks: give bicalutamide 50 mg daily starting ~1 week before and for ~4 weeks, especially with bone pain or cord risk
Goserelin (Zoladex)3.6 mg SC implant q28 days, or 10.8 mg q12 weeks
Triptorelin (Trelstar)3.75 mg IM q4 weeks, 11.25 mg q12 weeks, or 22.5 mg q24 weeks
Degarelix (Firmagon), antagonist240 mg SC loading (2 × 120 mg), then 80 mg SC q28 daysAs above; injection-site pain and lumpsNo flare: preferred when there is cord compression risk or urgent need
Relugolix (Orgovyx), oral antagonist360 mg PO on day 1, then 120 mg PO dailyAs above; diarrheaNo flare; testosterone recovers fast if doses are missed. P-gp interactions

Apalutamide (Erleada)240 mg PO dailyRash (can be severe), hypothyroidism, fatigue, falls and fractures, QTcStrong CYP3A4 inducer; check TSH; hold for grade 3 rash
Darolutamide (Nubeqa)600 mg PO BID with food (with ADT ± docetaxel in mHSPC)Fatigue, rash; fewer falls and CNS effects than enzalutamideFew drug interactions (raises rosuvastatin levels)
Bicalutamide (Casodex), first-generation50 mg PO daily (flare cover or combined with ADT)Breast enlargement and tenderness, hot flashes, raised LFTsMain inpatient use: flare protection when a GnRH agonist is started

Bone-targeted drugs

Drug (brand)Usual dose and scheduleCommon side effectsHospitalist pearls
Denosumab (Xgeva; Prolia)Bone mets (mCRPC): 120 mg SC q4 weeks. ADT bone loss: 60 mg SC q6 monthsLow calcium, joint pain, fatigue, osteonecrosis of the jawCalcium + vitamin D; OK in kidney failure but hypocalcemia is worse; rebound fractures if stopped without a follow-on drug

Other drugs you may see

  • Pembrolizumab 200 mg IV q3 weeks or 400 mg q6 weeks for MSI-high/dMMR tumours: immune side effects (colitis, pneumonitis, thyroid, adrenal).
  • Prednisone or dexamethasone with abiraterone, docetaxel or cabazitaxel, and for symptom control: glucose, mood, adrenal suppression.
  • Older hormone drugs (rare now): estrogens (VTE), ketoconazole (liver), cyproterone (liver, VTE).
