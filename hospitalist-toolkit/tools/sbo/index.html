CT signs of ischemia and closed loop

High-specificity signs: surgery now

  • Reduced or absent bowel wall enhancement (best single sign; needs IV contrast).
  • Pneumatosis intestinalis, portal or mesenteric venous gas (late, infarcted bowel).
  • Closed loop: U- or C-shaped dilated loop with two adjacent transition points, radial mesenteric vessels, beak sign.
  • Whirl sign: twisted mesentery (volvulus, internal hernia).
  • Hyperdense bowel wall on non-contrast CT (intramural hemorrhage).
  • Free air or extraluminal contrast: perforation.

Supporting signs: needs surgery to look today

  • Mesenteric edema or haziness, engorged mesenteric vessels.
  • Free fluid (more than a trace) between loops.
  • Bowel wall thickening over 3 mm, target sign.
  • Incarcerated hernia with fat stranding in the sac.

Signs that favour non-operative care

  • Small bowel feces sign, partial (low-grade) obstruction, contrast or gas reaching the colon.
CT sensitivity for ischemia is about 80-85%: a reassuring CT does not override peritonitis or a worsening patient.
Non-operative management
Water-soluble contrast challenge (Gastrografin)
When to operate

Surgery now (no trial of non-operative care)

  • Peritonitis, sepsis or shock attributed to the bowel.
  • CT signs of ischemia, closed loop, volvulus or perforation.
  • Incarcerated / strangulated hernia.
  • Lactate rising despite fluids, or worsening pain/tenderness on serial exams.

Surgery soon

  • No contrast in the colon 24 h after the water-soluble contrast challenge.
  • No resolution after 72 h of non-operative care (WSES), or recurrence on refeeding.
  • No previous surgery and no hernia ("virgin abdomen"): a cause needs to be found; low threshold for laparoscopy.

Usually non-operative first

  • Adhesive SBO without red flags (about 70-80% settle).
  • Early postoperative SBO (first 30 days), radiation enteritis, Crohn inflammatory stricture, diffuse carcinomatosis: with the surgeon's plan.

Medications while obstructed
Large bowel obstruction, volvulus and pseudo-obstruction
  • LBO: cancer (about 60%), volvulus, diverticular stricture. CT with IV contrast; competent ileocecal valve = closed loop, high perforation risk. Surgery vs stent by surgery/GI; no water-soluble contrast challenge.
  • Sigmoid volvulus: coffee-bean sign; flexible sigmoidoscopy detorsion + rectal tube if no ischemia, then resection this admission (recurrence about 60%). Cecal volvulus: surgery.
  • Ogilvie: treat causes (K, Mg, opioids, anticholinergics, infection), NG + rectal tube, mobilize. Cecum 12 cm or more or no better in 24-48 h: neostigmine 2 mg IV over 3-5 min on a monitor with atropine ready, or colonoscopic decompression.
  • Perforation risk: cecum 12 cm or more (LaPlace), duration over 6 days, tenderness over the cecum.
Malignant bowel obstruction
  • Surgery helps selected patients: single site, good performance status, no ascites, no diffuse carcinomatosis, prognosis of months. Stent for colonic or gastroduodenal single-level obstruction.
  • Medical: octreotide 100-200 mcg SC q8h (or 300-600 mcg/24 h infusion), dexamethasone 8 mg daily for 5 days (stop if no response), hyoscine butylbromide 20 mg SC q4-6h PRN for colic, haloperidol 0.5-1 mg SC q8h or ondansetron for nausea. Metoclopramide only if partial (no colic); stop if colic worsens.
  • Venting gastrostomy for persistent vomiting instead of a long-term NG. TPN only if prognosis is longer than about 2-3 months and performance status is reasonable.
  • Palliative care involved early; goals of care and place of care. See the Peritoneal carcinomatosis tab.
Daily monitoring
  • Abdominal exam every 4-6 h for the first 24-48 h (same examiner if possible): new peritonism or constant pain → surgery now.
  • Vitals q4h; HR and temperature trend. Strict intake/output, NG output each shift, urine output (catheter if unstable).
  • Lytes, creatinine, Mg, PO4 daily (more often while replacing K); lactate and CRP if the picture is changing.
  • Flatus / stool, distension, NG output volume and colour (bilious vs feculent).
  • NG removal: output under about 500 mL/24 h, not bilious, passing flatus: clamp 4-6 h; if no nausea and residual under about 200 mL, remove and start clear fluids.
  • VTE prophylaxis, mobilize, incentive spirometry, oral care; delirium watch in older patients.
