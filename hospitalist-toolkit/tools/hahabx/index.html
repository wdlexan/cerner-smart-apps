Cannot go in a 24-hour elastomeric – and workarounds
  • Meropenem: unstable at room/body temperature after ~4–6 h, so not a 24-h device. Locally, meropenem balls can be made – HaH pharmacy sets the device, duration and number of changes a day; confirm the nurses can do the changes.
  • Imipenem: unstable after ~4–6 h – no 24-h device.
  • ESBL E. coli / Enterobacterales without Pseudomonas: switch to ertapenem 1 g IV q24h (standard OPAT daily bolus).
  • Pseudomonas needing a carbapenem: meropenem balls per pharmacy (above), a programmable CADD pump giving q8h doses from a refrigerated cassette (if stability allows), switch to ceftazidime or piperacillin-tazobactam elastomeric if susceptible – otherwise inpatient care.
  • Ampicillin: short stability – use penicillin G elastomeric, ceftriaxone, or oral amoxicillin as the infection allows.
