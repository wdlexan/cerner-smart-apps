Acid-base: stepwise interpretation

Hospitalist Toolkit · Acid-base tab · SI units (mmol/L, g/L, mmHg) · normal: pH 7.35–7.45, pCO2 35–45, HCO3 22–26, anion gap 10 ± 2
✓ rule met: expected / single process, move to the next step✗ rule not met: a second process or a problem; do what the row says

1. Primary disorder: pH, pCO2, HCO3

Which way is the pH? The value that moved the same way as the pH is the primary process. Normal pH with abnormal values: lean on the side of 7.40 the pH sits.
FindingMeaning
✗pH < 7.35, HCO3 lowPrimary metabolic acidosis → step 2 (Winter's)
✗pH < 7.35, pCO2 highPrimary respiratory acidosis → step 2
✗pH > 7.45, HCO3 highPrimary metabolic alkalosis → step 2
✗pH > 7.45, pCO2 lowPrimary respiratory alkalosis → step 2
✗HCO3 low AND pCO2 high (or HCO3 high AND pCO2 low)Mixed: two primary processes pushing pH the same way
✓All three normalNo simple disorder, but still do step 3: a high anion gap can hide behind a normal pH
Consistency check: pH ≈ 6.1 + log(HCO3 ÷ (0.03 × pCO2)). Off by more than 0.05 → typo or values from different samples. Venous gas: pH about 0.03 lower, pCO2 about 4–5 higher; unreliable for pCO2 in shock.

Compensation never overshoots: if pH is on the "wrong" side of 7.40 for the primary process, there are two disorders. Renal compensation takes 3–5 days.

3. Anion gap (always, even with a normal pH)

AG = Na − (Cl + HCO3)   corrected AG + 0.25 × (40 − albumin g/L)   (each 10 g/L fall in albumin hides about 2.5)
✓Corrected AG ≤ 12Normal gap. If HCO3 is low → normal-gap (hyperchloraemic) acidosis → step 6a
✗Corrected AG > 12High anion gap metabolic acidosis present, whatever the pH → step 4 and 5, work through GOLDMARK
✗AG < 4Lab error, very low albumin, paraprotein (myeloma), lithium, bromide

4. Delta-delta (only when the gap is high)

Δ ratio = (corrected AG − 10) ÷ (24 − HCO3)
✗< 1High-gap acidosis + concurrent normal-gap acidosis (diarrhoea, saline, DKA recovery, CKD). < 0.4: mostly normal-gap
✓1 – 2Pure high-gap acidosis (DKA near 1, lactic acidosis near 1.6)
✗> 2, or high gap with HCO3 ≥ 24High-gap acidosis + concurrent metabolic alkalosis (vomiting, NG, diuretics) or baseline high HCO3 from chronic CO2 retention

5. Osmolar gap (high gap without a clear cause)

Osm gap = measured osmolality − (2 × Na + glucose + urea [+ ethanol])   all mmol/L, same sample
✓≤ 10Normal. Does NOT exclude a late toxic alcohol (parent alcohol already metabolised)
✗> 10 with high AGToxic alcohol until proven otherwise (methanol, ethylene glycol): fomepizole now, nephrology for dialysis, Poison Centre 1-800-567-8911
✗> 10 without high AGIsopropanol, ethanol not entered, mannitol, glycine, propylene glycol, severe lipids or protein

6a. Normal-gap acidosis workup: GI or kidney?

Urine AG = UNa + UK − UCl (spot urine; approximates urine NH4+)
✓Urine AG negativeKidneys excreting acid appropriately → GI loss of HCO3 (diarrhoea most common, ileostomy, pancreatic or biliary fistula) or saline load
✗Urine AG positiveRenal: renal tubular acidosis or CKD. Unreliable if urine Na < 20 or ketones / other unmeasured anions in urine
Low K + urine pH > 5.5Distal (type 1) RTA: ↓ acid excretion; Sjögren, lupus, amphotericin, lithium, stones
Low K + urine pH < 5.5Proximal (type 2) RTA: ↓ HCO3 reabsorption (myeloma, tenofovir, Fanconi), GI loss, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (acetazolamide, topiramate)
High KType 4 RTA (low aldosterone effect): diabetes, ACEi/ARB, spironolactone, trimethoprim, heparin, calcineurin inhibitors

6b. Metabolic alkalosis workup: urine chloride

✓Urine Cl < 20Chloride-responsive: vomiting, NG suction, past diuretics, post-hypercapnia, contraction → 0.9% saline + KCl, antiemetic
✗Urine Cl > 20Chloride-resistant: current diuretic, mineralocorticoid excess (check BP; renin/aldosterone), liquorice, severe low K, Bartter/Gitelman → treat cause, replace K and Mg
Use urine Cl rather than urine Na: bicarbonate in the urine drags Na with it even when volume-depleted.

High-gap causes: GOLDMARK (preferred)

  • Glycols: ethylene, propylene (IV lorazepam, phenobarbital)
  • Oxoproline: chronic acetaminophen + malnutrition/sepsis
  • L-lactate: shock, sepsis, ischaemia; metformin, liver failure, salbutamol, thiamine deficiency, isoniazid, iron, seizures
  • D-lactate: short bowel / bypass
  • Methanol
  • Aspirin (salicylate)
  • Renal failure (uraemia)
  • Ketoacidosis: diabetic (incl. euglycaemic on SGLT2i), alcoholic, starvation
First tests: lactate, beta-hydroxybutyrate, glucose, creatinine/urea, osmolality + ethanol (osmolar gap), salicylate, acetaminophen.

Older mnemonic: MUDPILES

  • Methanol · Uraemia · DKA
  • Propylene glycol (originally paraldehyde, now obsolete)
  • Isoniazid & iron (act through lactic acidosis)
  • Lactic acidosis · Ethylene glycol · Salicylate

Why GOLDMARK

  • Adds 5-oxoproline and D-lactate, which MUDPILES misses and which cause "unexplained" high gaps
  • Covers alcoholic and starvation ketoacidosis, not just DKA
  • Drops rare or indirect items (paraldehyde, isoniazid, iron) that are really lactic acidosis

Red flags: act now

  • pH < 7.1 or > 7.6; HCO3 < 10
  • Metabolic acidosis with pCO2 above Winter's range (tiring)
  • pCO2 > 60 with pH < 7.3: NIV / ICU, review opioids, O2 88–92%
  • SGLT2 inhibitor + metabolic acidosis: euglycaemic DKA (check BHB)
  • Lactate ≥ 4 or rising; metformin + high lactate + AKI
  • Osmolar gap > 10 + high anion gap
  • Respiratory alkalosis + high gap: send salicylate
  • K < 3 (replace before insulin or bicarbonate) or ≥ 6

Worked example (made-up patient A)

Venous pH 7.22, pCO2 22, HCO3 9; Na 137, Cl 102, albumin 38; on empagliflozin; glucose 11.2; BHB 5.8
  1. ✗ acidaemia, low HCO3 → metabolic acidosis
  2. ✓ Winter's 1.5 × 9 + 8 = 21.5 (19.5–23.5); pCO2 22 fits
  3. ✗ AG 137 − 111 = 26; corrected 26.5 → high gap
  4. ✓ Δ ratio 16.5 ÷ 15 = 1.1 → pure high gap
  5. GOLDMARK: K → euglycaemic DKA (SGLT2i); stop it, DKA protocol with dextrose
