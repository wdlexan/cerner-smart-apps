What it is

Ultrasound-guided percutaneous probe freezes a targeted nerve (about −60 to −88 °C) → second-degree axonotmesis / Wallerian degeneration. Epineurium and perineurium stay intact so the nerve regrows. No drug is injected and there is no maximum dose, so many nerves can be treated in one session. The Victoria (VGH) team adapted it from sensory nerves (pain) to motor and mixed nerves for spasticity in 2018.

Why choose it over botulinum toxin / phenol
  • Effect is immediate (visible on the table) and lasts longer — usually under one session per year.
  • No toxin dose ceiling, so large or many muscle groups can be treated in one visit.
  • No tachyphylaxis/antibody resistance; no chemical neurolysis fibrosis.
  • Reversible — nerve regrows, unlike surgical neurectomy.
Where it is unlikely to help
  • True fixed contracture — joint does not lengthen after lidocaine DNB. Needs serial casting / tendon lengthening / orthopedic or plastic surgery instead.
  • Generalized or severe multi-segment spasticity — oral agents or intrathecal baclofen fit better.
  • Muscles the patient relies on for transfers/standing (e.g. quadriceps "extensor tone" used to stand) — reducing tone may worsen function/falls.
  • Central or diffuse pain (central post-stroke pain, fibromyalgia, widespread pain) — no single peripheral nerve to target.
  • Pain that a diagnostic block does not relieve — predicts failure.
  • Acute hip fracture going to surgery — standard fascia iliaca / PENG block with local anesthetic is the routine choice; cryo-PENG pre-op is still in trials.
  • Motor nerve targets in patients who need preserved sensation — mixed nerves carry a higher dysesthesia risk.
Contraindications / cautions
  • AvoidCold urticaria, cryoglobulinemia, paroxysmal cold hemoglobinuria
  • AvoidInfection or open wound at the site
  • CautionBleeding disorder, anticoagulation, low platelets — discuss holding plan with proceduralist
  • CautionRaynaud phenomenon
  • CautionCannot cooperate / lie still — pediatric cases at VGH are done with sedation
Choose an indication
Usual pathway (VGH spasticity clinic)
  1. Referral → assessment (physiatry, with plastics/anesthesia).
  2. Ultrasound-guided lidocaine diagnostic nerve block(s): shows which muscles cause the problem, separates spasticity from contracture, confirms no sensory change, and lets the patient preview the result.
  3. Cryoneurolysis session (often outpatient, local anesthetic; sedation for children).
  4. Physio / stretching / bracing to use the window of reduced tone.
  5. Follow-up at ~1, 3, 6 and 12 months; repeat only if tone returns.

Hospitalist watch-list after a procedure
  • New falls risk after lower-limb targets (tibial, femoral, obturator) — PT reassessment, gait aid.
  • Insensate skin after mixed/sensory targets — skin/pressure checks, heel protection.
  • Hematoma if anticoagulated — check site, Hb if expanding.
  • Burning pain in the nerve territory in the weeks after — think post-cryo neuritis; contact the treating team.

What to include in the referral
  • Diagnosis and date of onset (stroke/TBI/SCI/MS/CP).
  • Specific goal: hygiene of the hand, brace fitting, gait, transfers, pain, seating.
  • Joints/muscles involved; Modified Ashworth if known; whether it lengthens.
  • Previous botulinum toxin (dose, response, duration), oral baclofen/tizanidine.
  • Anticoagulants/antiplatelets, platelets/INR; cold-related disorders.

Consult message (≈50 words)
Patient handout — after cryoneurolysis

What was done: a small probe froze a nerve to calm an overactive muscle or block pain. The nerve slowly regrows over months.

What to expect: some soreness at the site for a few days; the muscle may feel looser or weaker straight away; skin over the area may feel numb.

What to watch for:

  • Burning or electric pain, or new numbness, in the treated limb lasting more than a few days — call the clinic.
  • Leg feels weaker or you trip more — use your walker/cane and tell your physio.
  • Numb skin — check it daily for redness or sores, especially heels and hands.
  • Redness, warmth, swelling or pus at the needle site, or fever — see your doctor or clinic within 24 h.
  • If you take a blood thinner: a growing, tense, painful swelling at the site — go to the ER.

Call 911 if you have sudden weakness of the face/arm/leg on the other side, trouble speaking, chest pain or trouble breathing.
